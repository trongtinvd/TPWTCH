GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT
====================

Project Type
------------
Offline PvE 3D collectible/deck-building card game with a tactical board,
inspired by games such as Yu-Gi-Oh! and Magic: The Gathering.

Document Status
---------------
Working design document. Rules and implementation details may change during
development.


1. CORE GAME CONCEPT
====================

The game is an offline PvE card game centered around tactical combat,
positioning, card interactions, unusual stage mechanics, and opponent
adaptation.

The player selects a STORY to play ("enrage" a story). A story consists of
multiple stages. Each stage is a duel against an NPC, with narrative content
and potentially unique rules or gimmicks.

There is no permanent punishment for losing a duel. The player can retry a
stage.

The intended progression is:

    Story unlocked
        |
        v
    Stage / Duel
        |
        +--> Win -> progress
        |
        +--> Lose -> retry

Initially, one story is available. Completing stories unlocks additional
stories. The player can choose among unlocked stories and return to them
later.

Stages may contain explicit gimmicks. These gimmicks are listed in the stage
description rather than being hidden rules. The intended solution should be
discoverable, but brute-force and alternative solutions are allowed.


2. DESIGN GOALS
===============

Primary goals:

- Make positioning meaningful.
- Make card interactions highly composable.
- Allow unusual cards and NPC-exclusive mechanics.
- Make PvE opponents feel distinct rather than simply statistically stronger.
- Support AI opponents that reason about future game states.
- Make the rules engine deterministic and suitable for large-scale simulation.
- Separate game rules from 3D presentation.
- Allow the game to be expanded with many cards without requiring custom
  engine code for every card.
- Encourage players to understand and exploit stage-specific mechanics.
- Make losing informative enough that retrying feels worthwhile.


3. GAME BOARD
============

Each player has:

- Deck zone
- Extra Deck zone
- Hand
- Graveyard (GY)
- Exile zone
- Permanent zones

The permanent board consists of five columns.

Each column has four permanent zones:

    Opponent Back
    Opponent Front
    Player Front
    Player Back

Therefore the complete board has 20 permanent zones.

Current placement rules:

- Creatures occupy front-row zones.
- Non-creature permanents occupy back-row zones.
- A permanent normally requires an appropriate empty zone.
- Zone manipulation is binary: if a destination zone is occupied, movement
  normally fails/cancels.

The stack is a shared zone used for non-permanent cards and abilities.


4. PERMANENTS
=============

Permanent cards enter the field immediately and do not themselves enter the
stack.

Every permanent has HP while on the field.

A permanent dies when its HP reaches -1 or lower.

Permanent categories:

    Permanent
    |
    +-- Creature / Unit
    |
    +-- Non-creature Permanent

CREATURES

Creatures have:

- ATK
- HP
- Abilities
- Position

Only creatures can normally attack.

NON-CREATURE PERMANENTS

Non-creature permanents:

- Have HP.
- Do not have ATK by default.
- Cannot normally attack.
- Occupy back-row zones.


5. NON-PERMANENT CARDS
======================

Non-permanent cards enter the shared stack when played and never enter the
field.

Two major timing categories exist:

QUICK

Quick cards can be played whenever their timing restrictions permit.

SLOW

Slow cards can only be played during an open game state.

There is no universal mana system. Individual cards define their own costs,
restrictions, or requirements.

Quick cards can potentially be played multiple times during a turn if the
player can satisfy their individual requirements.


6. THE STACK
============

The stack follows a timing model inspired by Magic: The Gathering.

Stack objects include:

- Quick cards
- Slow cards while resolving/being processed
- Activated abilities
- Triggered abilities
- Other non-permanent effects

Players can respond to objects on the stack.

The general process is:

    Add object to stack
        |
        v
    Priority / responses
        |
        v
    Both players pass
        |
        v
    Resolve top object
        |
        v
    Trigger new effects
        |
        v
    Resume priority

A player can explicitly pass without responding.

A stack object has HP.

Stack HP represents how difficult it is to counter or destroy the object.
It does NOT directly scale the strength of the effect.

A stack object is countered when its HP reaches -1 or lower.

A stack object with exactly 0 HP still resolves normally.

This distinction is important for cards such as:

    Maximum Damage: Slow 0 HP

which can resolve despite having 0 HP.


7. TURN SYSTEM
==============

Each turn has exactly ONE major action.

The player may choose one:

    1. Draw two cards.
    2. Summon one permanent OR play one Slow spell.
    3. Enter combat.

Performing one major action ends the player's turn.

Other actions are generally free unless a card says otherwise.

Examples of free actions:

- Activate abilities.
- Use character abilities.
- Play Quick spells when legal.
- Inspect deck/GY when allowed.
- Perform card-specific actions.

There is no natural draw at the start of a turn.

Therefore drawing two cards requires spending the entire turn's major action.

The singleton deck structure is intended to limit repeated access to the same
cards and reduce infinite chaining.


8. TURN ORDER RESOURCE SYSTEM
=============================

Each player has:

- Initial Distance
- Distance
- Speed
- Acceleration

These values determine turn order and tempo.

At the beginning of the game:

    Distance = Initial Distance

The player currently farther ahead takes the next turn.

The player who just took a turn does NOT advance.

The player who did not receive the turn advances:

    Distance += current Speed
    Speed += Acceleration

Example:

    Distance = 10
    Speed = 3
    Acceleration = 2

After missing a turn:

    Distance = 13
    Speed = 5

If one player overtakes another, the overtaking player gets the next turn.
The player who missed the turn then advances according to the rules above.

This system can create consecutive turns / turn streaks.

If distances are tied, a coin toss determines who receives the turn.


9. PLAYER / CHARACTER
=====================

The player has the following primary attributes:

- Power
- Defense
- HP
- Initial Distance
- Speed
- Acceleration

POWER

Power is a pure multiplier to damage dealt by the player and their cards.

DEFENSE

Defense is a pure multiplier to damage taken by the player and their cards.

DISTANCE / SPEED / ACCELERATION

These govern the turn-order race.

Characters can modify attributes and provide unique abilities.

Characters are represented by unique 3D models.


10. EQUIPMENT
=============

Equipment can modify character or card statistics and abilities.

The game distinguishes three stat layers:

    Original Stat
        |
        v
    Base Stat
        |
        v
    Current Stat

ORIGINAL STAT

The printed stat of the card.

BASE STAT

A persistent modified version of the original stat.

CURRENT STAT

The stat currently used after temporary and equipment modifiers.

Example:

    Original creature: 1/1
    Base stat:         8/8
    Equipment:        +3/+4
    Current stat:     11/12

If the equipment leaves:

    Current stat -> 8/8

The base stat remains 8/8.

Base-stat modifications can persist when a card leaves the field, depending
on the effect's rules.


11. COMBAT
==========

Only creatures normally participate in combat.

A creature attacks the column in which it is located.

Damage travels through that column.

Resolution order:

    Attacking creature
          |
          v
    Opponent Front creature
          |
          v
    Opponent Back permanent
          |
          v
    Opponent Player

If the front-row target is absent, damage continues to the back-row target.

If the back-row target is also absent, remaining damage hits the player.

If the column is empty, the attack deals direct damage to the player.

Combat damage dealt by an attacker is based on its ATK.

The attacked creature does not automatically deal combat damage back to the
attacker.

Damage persists on permanents until removed or until the permanent leaves
the field.

Combat is a major action and ends the turn.


12. DAMAGE SYSTEM
==================

Damage should be represented as first-class objects rather than only as a
single accumulated integer.

Conceptual model:

    DamageInstance
        - amount
        - source
        - type
        - timestamp
        - tags

A permanent's current damage can be calculated as the sum of its active
damage instances.

This allows cards to interact with individual damage events.

Example:

    Creature has 8 total damage.

    Damage instance A = 7
    Damage instance B = 1
    Damage instance B was dealt by Avatar of Rabies.

A card such as Throwing Bandages can choose which damage instances to remove.

Damage types may include:

- Combat damage
- Effect damage
- Other card-defined types

Different systems can react differently to different damage types.


13. ZONE SYSTEM
===============

Zones are first-class game objects rather than passive board coordinates.

A zone may have:

- Occupancy
- Position
- Usability
- Restrictions
- Integrity / HP
- Effects
- Ownership
- Special rules

A centralized zone-rule system should answer questions such as:

    CanEnterZone(card, zone)
    CanTargetZone(source, zone)
    CanMoveToZone(card, zone)
    IsZoneUsable(zone)

This avoids scattering special-case checks throughout cards and systems.


14. COLUMN SYSTEM
=================

Columns are first-class objects because several mechanics operate on an
entire column.

A column can answer:

    IsUsable()
    IsFullyOccupied()
    ExileEverything()
    GetZones()

A column is fully filled when all four permanent zones in that column are
occupied:

    Opponent Back
    Opponent Front
    Player Front
    Player Back

A column can become unusable for the rest of the game.

The exact meaning of an unusable column should be defined centrally rather
than by individual cards.


15. ZONE INTEGRITY
==================

Some effects can give zones their own HP/integrity.

Example:

    Fracture Ground

Each relevant zone gains 10 HP.

Zone integrity is separate from permanent HP.

Regular combat does not affect zone integrity.

When a zone reaches 0 or lower:

    - Exile cards on the zone.
    - Mark the zone unusable.

Zone integrity should be implemented as a zone-level effect/restriction,
rather than as a special one-off GameState variable.

Stacking behavior for multiple zone-integrity effects must be explicitly
defined.


16. EFFECT SYSTEM
=================

The game should avoid implementing every unusual card as custom hard-coded
logic.

Instead, cards should be composed from a small set of reusable effects.

Major effect categories:

    One-shot effects
    Triggered effects
    Continuous effects
    Rule / restriction effects

Example primitive operations:

    DealDamage
    Destroy
    Exile
    Move
    Summon
    Draw
    ModifyStat
    ModifyRule
    AddAbility
    RemoveAbility
    CreateTrigger
    CreateDelayedEffect
    CreateChoice
    WaitForInput

Complex cards are compositions of these primitives.

The game should preserve the wrapper-like behavior the design uses for rare
effects, but the implementation should preferably use an Effect/Modifier
system that registers rule changes rather than literally wrapping the entire
GameState in nested decorators.


17. EFFECT QUERIES
==================

Important centralized queries include:

    CanEnterZone
    GetEffectiveStat
    GetEffectiveDistance
    GetEffectiveAbilities
    GetVisibleInformation
    CanDealDamage
    CanTarget
    CanMove
    CanAttack

The underlying GameState remains the source of game truth.

Queries calculate the effective state after applying relevant modifiers.

This avoids adding many one-off booleans such as:

    disabledColumn
    creatureCannotMove
    playerCannotDraw
    hiddenCard
    etc.

Temporary effects can instead register rules that modify the relevant query.


18. MODIFIER PRIORITY
=====================

Because multiple effects can contradict one another, the modifier system
needs explicit precedence rules.

Potential structure:

    Base Rule
        |
    Persistent Modifiers
        |
    Temporary Modifiers
        |
    Specific Overrides
        |
    Final Effective Result

The exact ordering should be formalized before a large card library is
implemented.

Example conflicts:

    Effect A: creatures cannot move.
    Effect B: this creature can move.
    Effect C: this column cannot be entered.
    Effect D: this creature ignores movement restrictions.

The engine must have deterministic answers.


19. ABILITIES
============

Abilities should be separated conceptually into:

    Original abilities
    Granted abilities
    Disabled/removed abilities
    Effective abilities

An ability should not normally be physically deleted from a card merely
because an effect temporarily removes it.

Example:

    Avatar of Rabies

Damage dealt by Avatar can cause the target to lose its original ability and
gain Avatar of Rabies's abilities.

A layered ability system makes this possible without destroying the original
card definition.


20. TRIGGERS AND EVENTS
=======================

The simulation should expose deliberate gameplay events.

Potential events include:

    TurnStarted
    TurnEnded
    PermanentEntered
    PermanentLeft
    CreatureAttackDeclared
    AttackStarted
    DamageAttempted
    DamageDealt
    DamageReceived
    CreatureDestroyed
    CardDrawn
    CardPlayed
    StackObjectCreated
    StackObjectResolved

Events should represent simulation facts, not visual animation events.

For example:

    CreatureAttackDeclared

is a rules event.

An animation such as:

    AttackAnimationStarted

should generally remain in presentation unless a specific mechanic requires
real-time interaction.


21. TARGETING
=============

Targeting should be a first-class system.

Possible target categories:

    Card
    Permanent
    Creature
    Zone
    Column
    Player
    Stack Object
    Damage Instance

Target specifications should support restrictions and dynamic queries.

Example:

    The Vine

does not store a fixed target. It asks the engine for the closest legal
zone according to its own distance function.

The target should be recalculated on each repetition because the board can
change between repetitions.


22. INFORMATION / HIDDEN INFORMATION
====================================

The actual GameState contains complete game truth.

Players and AI should receive filtered views of that truth.

Conceptual model:

    GameState
        |
        v
    Information / Visibility System
        |
        v
    Player Observation

Cards such as King Eye should modify what an opponent can discern without
deleting or corrupting the underlying card information.

"Cannot discern" should eventually be formally defined, potentially covering:

- Existence
- Location
- Identity
- Type
- Stats
- Abilities
- Equipment
- Other relevant information

Visual effects such as making the board appear black are presentation-layer
consequences of the information system.


23. REAL-TIME INPUT EFFECTS
===========================

Some mechanics intentionally interact with real-time player input.

Example:

    Parriable

The simulation should not directly poll Godot input.

Instead:

    Simulation
        |
        v
    Input Window Requested
        |
        v
    Presentation displays prompt
        |
        v
    PlayerCommand
        |
        v
    Simulation resolves command

The simulation may enter states such as:

    Resolved
    WaitingForInput
    WaitingForChoice

This keeps real-time presentation separate from the rules.

If replays or deterministic simulation are required, player commands and
relevant timing information should be recorded.


24. EXAMPLE CARD: THE VINE
==========================

Creature 1/1

Effect:

"When this card enters and at the beginning of your turn, apply the following
effect 4 times. Target the closest permanent zone to this card that isn't
occupied by this card or a Vine token (you choose if ties), destroy all cards
on that zone and create a Vine creature 1/1 on that tile."

Implementation concept:

    Trigger:
        PermanentEntered(self)

    Trigger:
        TurnStarted(controller)

    On trigger:
        Repeat 4:
            Find closest legal zone
            Destroy all cards on zone
            Create Vine token

Each repetition re-evaluates the board.

If no legal zone exists, that repetition does nothing.

The card defines its own mathematical distance function. The engine supplies
candidate zones and legality information.

Important unresolved rule:
If a Vine is a creature and creatures are restricted to front-row zones,
the game must define whether this effect can create a Vine in any targeted
zone or whether only legal creature zones can be selected.


25. EXAMPLE CARD: INFINITY WALL
===============================

Quick 6 HP

"Target a column that is fully filled, exile all cards on that column, that
column becomes unusable for the rest of the game. Return this card to your
hand."

Implementation:

    Check target column
    Check IsFullyOccupied()
    Exile contents
    Apply permanent column restriction
    Return Infinity Wall to hand

The column restriction should be implemented through the rule/modifier
system rather than a one-off GameState variable.


26. EXAMPLE CARD: FRACTURE GROUND
==================================

Slow 4 HP

"Each zone gains 10 HP (they are unaffected by regular combat). When those
zone die, exile the cards on that zone, it becomes unusable."

Implementation:

    Apply ZoneIntegrity(+10)
    Apply CombatDamageImmunityToZoneIntegrity()
    On ZoneIntegrity <= 0:
        Exile contents
        Mark zone unusable

Important unresolved rule:
Define whether "each zone" means only permanent zones or every zone in the
game, including deck, hand, GY, exile, and stack.


27. EXAMPLE CARD: MAXIMUM DAMAGE
=================================

Slow 0 HP

"For the next three turns, your distance, speed, and acceleration are
treated as 0. When the effect ends, destroy all cards your opponent controls,
you draw 7 cards, and gain life equal to the total HP + total attack of all
the destroyed cards."

Implementation:

    Continuous modifier:
        EffectiveDistance = 0
        EffectiveSpeed = 0
        EffectiveAcceleration = 0

    Duration:
        Three turns

    On expiration:
        Destroy opponent-controlled cards
        Snapshot their relevant HP/ATK
        Draw 7
        Gain corresponding life

The underlying distance/speed/acceleration values remain unchanged.

The exact timing boundary for "next three turns" must be defined.


28. EXAMPLE CARD: KING EYE
==========================

Creature 6/10

"Your Opponent cannot discern other cards you control."

This is an information-system effect.

The underlying board remains unchanged.

The opponent receives a restricted observation.

Presentation may represent this by obscuring the relevant board.

The information restriction should be reusable by other cards.


29. EXAMPLE CARD: AVATAR OF RABIES
===================================

Creature 3/7

"Whenever a creature attacks, you may shift this card to an adjacent column.
Whenever this card takes combat damage it deals 1 damage to another creature.
Damage dealt by this card has 'This card loses all of its original ability
and gains the abilities of Avatar of Rabies'."

Implementation:

    On CreatureAttackDeclared:
        Optional choice:
            Move left
            Move right
            Stay

    On CombatDamageReceived:
        Deal 1 damage to another creature

Movement uses normal zone legality.

Damage-trigger behavior should be based on damage events rather than
individual damage points unless the card rules explicitly say otherwise.

The final clause should be implemented as an ability transformation effect,
not by permanently deleting the target's card definition.


30. EXAMPLE CARD: THROWING BANDAGES
====================================

Quick 10 HP

"Remove 7 damage counters from any target."

The player chooses which damage instances to remove.

Example:

    Damage A = 7
    Damage B = 1

The player can remove the 1-point instance and six points from another
instance if permitted by the exact damage-counter definition.

The game should explicitly decide whether "7 damage counters" means seven
individual points or a total damage amount of seven.


31. EXAMPLE CARD: THE SEVEN STEVES
===================================

Slow 7 HP

"Exile 7 creatures from your field or GY, each of those creatures deals
damage to any non-player target."

Implementation:

    Select 7 creature instances
    Exile them
    Snapshot selected creatures
    Each selected creature deals its specified damage

The card text must define the amount of damage if it is not otherwise
specified by the game.

Card instances must remain independently identifiable when moved between
zones.


32. EXAMPLE CARD: PARRIABLE
============================

Slow 2 HP

"Your opponent gains 'Power double, any damage you deal may miss if you do
not press Enter within 2 seconds before the attack animation lands'. You gain
'Whenever your opponent would deal damage, you may press Enter once to create
0.5 second of parry, if your opponent presses Enter within that window negate
the damage'."

This requires real-time input windows.

Recommended model:

    DamageEvent
        |
        v
    ParryWindowRequested
        |
        v
    Presentation input prompt
        |
        v
    AttemptParry(windowID)
        |
        v
    Simulation resolves damage

The temporary granted abilities should be represented as first-class effects.

The exact ownership, timing, and interaction of simultaneous input windows
must be defined.


33. CARD DEFINITION PHILOSOPHY
==============================

Cards should preferably be data-driven.

A card definition should describe:

- Name
- Type
- Printed stats
- Timing class
- Costs
- Conditions
- Targets
- Triggers
- Effects
- Tags
- Rules text
- Visual references

Card instances contain mutable runtime state.

Card definitions should not contain per-instance state.


34. CARD INSTANCE VS CARD DEFINITION
=====================================

Conceptual separation:

    CardDefinition
        |
        | creates
        v
    CardInstance

CardDefinition contains immutable design data.

CardInstance contains runtime state such as:

- Current zone
- Damage
- Base stat modifications
- Current modifiers
- Attachments
- Counters
- Temporary effects
- Instance ID

This is especially important for:

- Repeated copies of the same card
- Tokens
- Cards moving through zones
- AI simulations
- Replays
- Save games

36. SIMULATION / PRESENTATION SEPARATION
=========================================

The simulation should not depend on Godot Node3D objects, meshes, animations,
cameras, or visual effects.

The simulation contains the truth.

The presentation observes simulation results and displays them.

Conceptual flow:

    Player Command
        |
        v
    Simulation
        |
        v
    Events
        |
        v
    Presentation
        |
        v
    Animation / VFX / UI

This allows the same simulation to power:

- Human matches
- AI matches
- Headless simulations
- Replays
- Automated testing
- Balance testing
- AI tournaments


37. COMMANDS AND EVENTS
=======================

Commands represent intent.

Examples:

    Draw
    Summon
    PlayCard
    Attack
    ActivateAbility
    ChooseTarget
    PassPriority
    MovePermanent
    AttemptParry

Events represent facts.

Examples:

    CardDrawn
    PermanentSummoned
    AttackDeclared
    DamageDealt
    PermanentDestroyed
    CardExiled
    AbilityActivated

The distinction is useful for:

- debugging
- replay
- AI
- UI
- animations
- automated tests


38. DETERMINISM
==============

The simulation should preferably be deterministic given:

    Initial GameState
    +
    Player Commands
    +
    RNG Seed

This enables:

- reproducible bugs
- deterministic AI tests
- replays
- tournament analysis
- balance experiments

Random decisions should be controlled by the simulation's RNG rather than
random calls scattered throughout presentation code.


39. AI SYSTEM
=============

The intended AI concept is a simulation-based planner.

The AI:

1. Observes the game.
2. Estimates hidden information.
3. Generates plausible hidden game states.
4. Simulates legal actions.
5. Simulates opponent responses.
6. Evaluates resulting states.
7. Chooses a plan.
8. Executes the first action.
9. Re-plans after the real game state changes.

Architecture:

    AI
    |
    +-- BeliefModel
    |
    +-- Planner
    |
    +-- PersonalityEvaluator


40. AI BELIEF MODEL
===================

The AI should not receive hidden information directly.

Instead:

    Actual GameState
        |
        v
    AI Observation
        |
        v
    BeliefModel
        |
        v
    Possible Worlds
        |
        v
    Planner

The belief model estimates things such as:

- Probability of cards in opponent hand.
- Probability of cards remaining in deck.
- Likelihood of responses.
- Known information.
- Unknown information.

Rather than enumerating every possible hidden hand, the AI can sample
plausible possible worlds.

Example:

    Counter card probability = 70%

The AI can evaluate:

    World A: counter exists
    World B: counter does not exist

weighted by their estimated probabilities.


41. AI SEARCH
============

The planner should be replaceable.

Possible planners:

    IPlanner
        +-- GreedyPlanner
        +-- BeamSearchPlanner
        +-- DeterminizedSearchPlanner
        +-- MCTSPlanner
        +-- ISMCTSPlanner

Do not commit the entire architecture to one search algorithm too early.

The one-major-action-per-turn structure naturally reduces some branching, but
free actions and card interactions can still create large search trees.


42. AI EVALUATION
=================

The AI should evaluate resulting game states, not only individual actions.

Conceptually:

    Score =
        StateEvaluation
        +
        EventEvaluation

Possible personality weights:

    PlayerDamage
    CreatureCount
    CreaturePower
    CardAdvantage
    BoardControl
    PlayerHP
    OwnHP
    Risk
    EmptyZones
    LethalThreat
    etc.

Example personality:

    Damage player = +2
    Summon creature = +8
    Draw cards = +3
    Take damage = -1

Another personality:

    Damage player = +4
    Summon creature = +3
    Draw cards = +8
    Take damage = -20

The same game state can therefore produce different preferred actions.


43. AI PERSONALITY
==================

Personality is represented as a vector of preferences rather than only a
named archetype.

Possible archetypes include:

- Aggressive
- Defensive
- Control
- Opportunistic
- Risk-taking
- Resource-focused
- Board-focused

However, actual personalities can be continuous combinations of weights.

This allows many NPCs to have distinct behavior without requiring unique AI
algorithms.


44. AI SEARCH OPTIMIZATION
==========================

Do not initially attempt exhaustive search of every legal sequence.

Use:

- Action pruning
- Heuristic pre-evaluation
- Beam search
- Limited hidden-world samples
- Search depth limits
- Time budgets
- Transposition tables
- State hashing
- Re-planning

Example branching:

    20 actions
    x 15 responses
    x 15 actions
    x 15 responses

can already produce approximately 67,500 simulated branches.

Therefore simulation speed and search pruning are important.


45. AI DIFFICULTY
=================

AI difficulty can be controlled without changing its rules.

Possible difficulty parameters:

- Search depth
- Beam width
- Number of possible-world samples
- Time budget
- Evaluation noise
- Planning frequency
- Hidden-information confidence
- Number of candidate actions considered

A weaker AI can use the same architecture while simply searching less or
making less precise evaluations.


46. AI TOURNAMENT SYSTEM
========================

A development-only simulation framework can run large numbers of AI matches.

Architecture:

    Simulation Framework
    |
    +-- Game Simulation
    +-- AI Planner
    +-- AI Tournament
    +-- Personality Generator
    +-- Statistics Collector
    +-- Replay
    +-- Balance Testing

Possible metrics:

- Win rate
- Game length
- Turns
- Damage dealt
- Damage taken
- Cards drawn
- Cards played
- Creatures summoned
- Creatures destroyed
- Spells/abilities used
- Board value
- Lethal opportunities
- Missed lethal opportunities
- Risky plays
- Defensive plays
- Strategic diversity


47. PERSONALITY SEARCH / EVOLUTION
===================================

Personalities can be automatically generated and tested.

Possible process:

    Generate population
          |
          v
    Run tournaments
          |
          v
    Measure strength + quality
          |
          v
    Select promising personalities
          |
          v
    Mutate weights
          |
          v
    Repeat

Do not optimize only for win rate.

A personality that wins almost every game may be strategically boring.

Fitness can include:

    WinRate
    +
    GameQuality
    +
    StrategicVariety
    +
    Distinctiveness
    +
    AppropriateDifficulty


48. SELF-PLAY OVERFITTING
=========================

AI self-play can overfit to the opponents it encounters.

Tournament populations should therefore include:

- Current personalities
- Historical personalities
- Random personalities
- Hand-designed benchmark AIs
- Different decks
- Potentially recorded human strategies

The goal is to discover robust strategies rather than strategies that only
defeat one particular opponent population.


49. HEADLESS SIMULATION
=======================

The simulation should be capable of running without rendering.

Headless simulation is important for:

- AI development
- Balance testing
- Personality tournaments
- Regression testing
- Large-scale game generation

A useful development benchmark is:

    "How many complete simulated turns per second can the target PC run?"

This should be measured before aggressively optimizing the architecture.


50. REPLAY SYSTEM
=================

A replay can potentially be represented as:

    Initial State / Decks
    +
    RNG Seed
    +
    Player Commands

The simulation can then reproduce the game.

This is useful for:

- Debugging
- AI analysis
- Tournament review
- Player replays
- Finding balance problems
- Reproducing rare interactions


51. RULES TESTING
=================

Every major rule should have automated tests.

Examples:

- Combat damage correctly tramples through front row.
- Empty columns deal direct damage.
- Dead permanents leave the field at <= -1 HP.
- 0 HP stack objects resolve.
- -1 HP stack objects are countered.
- Zone restrictions block illegal movement.
- Column restrictions affect all intended operations.
- Base stats persist correctly.
- Equipment modifies current stats without modifying base stats.
- Temporary modifiers expire correctly.
- Hidden information is not exposed to AI.
- Simulated games match real-game outcomes.


52. CORE DESIGN RISKS
=====================

RISK 1: TOO MANY INDEPENDENT SYSTEMS

The game currently contains:

- Positioning
- Stack interaction
- HP on cards
- Zone HP
- Persistent base stats
- Distance/speed/acceleration
- Hidden information
- Real-time input
- Stage gimmicks
- AI prediction

Each system is individually interesting, but their combined complexity can
become difficult to teach and balance.

The game needs a clear central skill identity.

RISK 2: RULE INTERACTION EXPLOSION

A large card library will eventually produce interactions that were not
anticipated.

The answer should be a strong generic rules/effect system rather than
hundreds of card-specific exceptions.

RISK 3: MODIFIER ORDER

Conflicting temporary and permanent effects need deterministic precedence.

RISK 4: AI COMPUTATION

Hidden information plus look-ahead can multiply the number of simulations.

Sampling, pruning, beam search, and time budgets are likely to be necessary.

RISK 5: REAL-TIME MECHANICS

Real-time input can conflict with deterministic turn-based simulation if the
boundary between presentation and simulation is not carefully designed.

RISK 6: INFORMATION COMPLEXITY

The game can become difficult to understand if too many cards simultaneously
modify rules, zones, visibility, stats, and timing.


53. CORE DESIGN QUESTIONS TO RESOLVE
=====================================

Before the card pool becomes large, define:

1. What exactly does "destroy" mean compared with other ways of leaving play?
2. Which stat modifications persist through which zone transitions?
3. What exactly does "unusable zone" prevent?
4. What exactly does "unusable column" prevent?
5. What does "cannot discern" reveal or hide?
6. How are simultaneous triggers ordered?
7. How are mandatory and optional triggers ordered?
8. What wins when modifiers contradict each other?
9. What exactly counts as a damage counter/instance?
10. How do zone HP effects stack?
11. Exactly when does a multi-turn effect expire?
12. What happens to temporary effects when their host changes zones?
13. Which events are simulation events versus presentation events?
14. What is the exact definition of legal targeting?
15. How are generated tokens represented?
16. Can cards create creatures in normally illegal zones?
17. What exactly constitutes the end of an effect?
18. What is the game's primary strategic skill?

55. GUIDING ARCHITECTURE
========================

The most important architectural principle is:

    GAME RULES ARE DATA + SIMULATION,
    NOT GODOT PRESENTATION CODE.

A strong conceptual dependency direction is:

    Content
       |
       v
    Simulation
       |
       v
    Events / Observations
       |
       v
    Presentation

AI should interact with the same Simulation.

Therefore:

    Human
       \
        \
         -> Simulation
        /
       /
    AI

and not:

    Human -> Godot-specific rules
    AI   -> separate rules implementation

The same simulation should be authoritative for both.


56. LONG-TERM VISION
====================

The intended mature architecture supports:

- Large card libraries
- Complex interactions
- NPC-exclusive mechanics
- Distinct AI personalities
- Hidden-information reasoning
- Deep look-ahead
- Automated AI tournaments
- Personality discovery
- Deterministic replays
- Headless simulation
- Automated regression tests
- Rich 3D presentation
- Stage-specific PvE mechanics

The central technical objective is to make the simulation sufficiently clean
and generic that increasingly strange cards can be added by composing existing
rules rather than rewriting the engine.

The central design objective is to make those complex systems produce
meaningful decisions rather than complexity for its own sake.
